## I have an idea for an app. What do I do first?

Before you think about code, find out whether the problem your app would solve hurts enough for someone to pay to make it stop. Write the idea in two sentences: who it is for, and what it saves them. Then talk to ten people who fit that description, and do not pitch. Ask how they handle the problem today, what it costs them in time or money, and what they have already tried. If most of them shrug, you just saved yourself a year. If three or four lean in and ask when they can use it, you have something worth building. This step costs a few coffees, and it is the easiest one to skip, because hiring a developer feels like more progress than talking to strangers.

## Do I need to learn to code or find a technical cofounder?

No, and starting with either one is how a lot of good ideas stall. Learning to code well enough to ship a real product takes many months of steady study. Searching for a technical cofounder can take just as long, and it usually means handing a large share of your company to someone you met recently. You have three practical routes instead. No-code tools let you assemble a simple version yourself, which is enough to test demand. A freelancer can build a clearly defined piece of work, as long as you are willing to manage the project. A small development studio designs, builds and launches the whole product, which costs more but puts one team in charge of the result. Choose the route that fits how much of the project you want to run yourself, and let price decide only between options that fit.

## How much does it cost to build an app in 2026?

A basic MVP costs roughly $10,000 to $50,000, according to cost guides from development shops such as [Designli](https://designli.co/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-an-mvp) and [Techtic](https://www.techtic.com/blog/mvp-development-cost-2026-pricing-breakdown/). Products in regulated fields like health or finance cost far more. Three things move the number most: how many kinds of users the app serves, how many outside services (like payments or maps) it connects to, and whether it has to live in the app stores on day one or can start as a web app. Budget for what comes after launch, too. Hosting, a database and email add a monthly bill, Apple charges [$99 a year](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/) to publish on the App Store, and Google Play asks for a [one-time $25 fee](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435).

## How do I get my app made without wasting money?

Shrink the first version until it does one job completely. If your idea is a marketplace for dog walkers, version one lets an owner book a walker and pay. No ratings, no chat, no loyalty points. Then ask four questions before you sign with anyone. Is the scope written down, feature by feature? Is there a delivery date? Will I own the source code from the first day, in an account I control? Will I see working progress every week, rather than one demo at the end? If a developer cannot give you a clear answer to all four, keep looking, however good the price sounds.

## How do I turn my app idea into a reality?

Spend the next seven days turning the idea into evidence. Write your two sentences, have your ten conversations, and sketch the one job your first version must do on a single page. If the idea survives that, get a clickable preview and a written estimate of what the product will cost to build and to run before you commit months and a budget to it.
